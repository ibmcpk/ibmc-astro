This investigation looks at how surface area changes the time something takes. You change the surface area and measure the time. Then you plot the data and fit a line to find the pattern. Many processes, like cooling or drying, follow a power rule. This method helps you see if the data match the rule you expected.

### Personal Interests & Hypotheses

1. **Parachute** — Interest in skydiving or model rockets. *Hypothesis:* Bigger canopy → longer fall time.
2. **Evaporation** — Noticing puddles dry faster when spread out. *Hypothesis:* Bigger surface → shorter drying time.
3. **Paper helicopter** — Making paper toys and racing them. *Hypothesis:* Fall time scales with wing area.
4. **Cooling** — Wanting tea to cool faster; flat plate vs. bowl. *Hypothesis:* Bigger surface → shorter cooling time.
5. **Dissolving** — Sugar cubes vs. loose sugar in tea. *Hypothesis:* More surface → faster dissolving

**Common thread:** Everyday moments—cooking, toys, laundry, drinks—where surface area quietly changes how long things take.
