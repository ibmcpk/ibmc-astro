## Cost of Transporting Tomatoes: Replacing bad guesses

### Introduction

This investigation suggests using order-of-magnitude estimates to find out how much of a tomato's price comes from transporting it by truck. Many people guess by eye and get the answer wrong. A better way is to estimate the truck's volume and one tomato's volume, then divide. All numbers are rounded to the nearest power of ten, because the goal is to be within a factor of ten, not to be exact. This shows how a simple estimate can replace a bad guess.

### Personal Interests & Hypotheses

1. **Grocery shopping** — I noticed that local and imported tomatoes cost about the same, which made me wonder how big the transport cost really is. *Hypothesis:* Transport adds only a small share to the price, under about 10%.
2. **Truck spotting** — Watching delivery trucks on the highway made me curious how many tomatoes one truck can carry. *Hypothesis:* A truck holds on the order of 10⁵ tomatoes.
3. **Bad guesses** — I have seen people guess wildly wrong at "how many in the jar" contests. *Hypothesis:* Guessing by eye will be off by more than a factor of ten, while estimating from linear dimensions will land within one order of magnitude.
4. **Food prices** — Wanting to know why fresh produce is cheap in some places and costly in others. *Hypothesis:* Transport cost per tomato will be about 2 cents, far below the tomato's own price.
5. **Rounding to powers of ten** — Curious how far a rough, rounded estimate can go. *Hypothesis:* Rounding the bin to 10 m³ and a tomato to 10⁻⁴ m³ will still give a total within a factor of ten of the true count.

**Common thread:** Curiosity about everyday prices, trucks, and how rough math can beat a wild guess.
