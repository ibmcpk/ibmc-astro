This investigation can model the surface area and volume of a lake or reservoir. Two methods can be used. The first treats the lake as one simple shape. The second splits it into smaller shapes. The results are checked against published values. This shows how good a simple math model works, and how adding detail changes the answer. The work uses coordinates, area formulas, and volume formulas, all tied to real map data.

### Personal Interests & Hypotheses

1. **Satellite maps** — I like zooming in on lakes and coastlines and checking my own measurements. *Hypothesis:* Map-based measurements will land within about 10% of published official values.
2. **Geography class** — Seeing a huge lake on a map made me want to know its real size and depth. *Hypothesis:* Volume ÷ surface area will give a mean depth close to the recorded mean depth.
3. **Scale models** — Curious how a 3D shape can be split into simple rectangles and trapezoids. *Hypothesis:* A model with more shapes will give a surface area closer to the real value than a single-shape model.
4. **Comparing methods** — Which is better: a quick one-shape estimate or a careful shape-by-shape model? *Hypothesis:* Surface area and volume will grow together, and their ratio will show the lake's average depth.
5. **Water and the environment** — Interest in lakes, water supply, and how much water a lake holds. *Hypothesis:* Depths sampled across the lake will give a reliable average, and volume will depend more on area than on depth.

**Common thread:** Curiosity about maps, real-world scale, and how good a simple model can be.
