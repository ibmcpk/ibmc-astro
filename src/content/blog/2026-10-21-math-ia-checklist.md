## IB Math IA Criteria Checklist

### Criterion A: Presentation

- A.1 — Work is divided into sections: introduction, body, and conclusion
- A.2 — Body of work is further subdivided so phases of exploration are clearly indicated
- A.3 — Topic of the IA is stated clearly and explained in the introduction
- A.4 — Introduction includes general description of approach and what area of math curriculum the exploration focuses on
- A.5 — Conclusion is a summary of outcomes and a response to the aim stated in the introduction
- A.6 — Exploration is clear and understandable throughout (no rereading of sections necessary)
- A.7 — Consequent parts of the work are linked logically (one follows up the other)
- A.8 — Use of technology, when applied, is clearly indicated and explained
- A.9 — If analytical software is used, its purpose is described and link provided
- A.10 — Graphs, tables, and diagrams are present in appropriate places (in body paragraphs, after descriptive introduction)
- A.11 — Only large tables (raw data) or additional diagrams/graphs are attached as appendices
- A.12 — Exploration is 12–20 pages long (excluding bibliography and appendices)
- A.13 — Font is 12pt, double spacing used, text justified
- A.14 — All information outside syllabus scope is referenced (footnotes or in-text citations)
- A.15 — All mathematical formulae, equations, and other calculations are centered
- A.16 — Pages numbered in bottom right corner (page count starts with introduction on first page)
- A.17 — No unnecessary or repetitive calculations/graphs

---

### Criterion B: Mathematical Communication

- B.1 — Correct mathematical notation, symbols, and terminology used consistently and correctly
- B.2 — Computer notation present only if software-generated (clear indication necessary)
- B.3 — Key terms/variables defined and explained when first introduced
- B.4 — Main concepts described in the introduction
- B.5 — Additional terms defined in detail in appropriate places
- B.6 — Multiple forms of mathematical representation present but used only if appropriate (formulae, diagrams, tables, charts, graphs, models)
- B.7 — All mathematical calculations explained and any data presentation described
- B.8 — Degree of accuracy stated for any rounded values
- B.9 — Results rounded only at the end of the calculations thread
- B.10 — Deductive method used and logical proofs set out where appropriate
- B.11 — Graphs, tables, and diagrams labeled (titled and numbered) at the bottom

---

### Criterion C: Personal Engagement

- C.1 — Link between topic of exploration and student's personal interests is described
- C.2 — Questions posed in introduction and latter part of exploration, with answers found over the course of the investigation
- C.3 — Predictions made and tested, with evaluation present
- C.4 — Exploration looks at topic from different perspectives
- C.5 — Exploration includes models/indices/formulae devised or modified by the student

---

### Criterion D: Reflection

- D.1 — Exploration includes final evaluation
- D.2 — Evaluation discusses strengths and weaknesses of the exploration as well as possibilities for improvement
- D.3 — Evaluation contains ideas for further investigations linked to the work
- D.4 — Multiple perspectives presented in the exploration are compared and contrasted
- D.5 — Limitations of the exploration are considered
- D.6 — Accuracy/effectiveness of different approaches/models is compared
- D.7 — Comments on implications of intermediate findings present throughout the exploration
- D.8 — Comment on accuracy of calculations is present
- D.9 — Evaluation links back to the aims of the exploration

---

### Criterion E: Use of Mathematics

- E.1 — Overly complicated mathematics not used when unnecessary
- E.2 — Mathematics used is part of the syllabus or at a similar level
- E.3 — Any mathematics outside syllabus scope is explained
- E.4 — Understanding demonstrated without shortcuts; every step of calculations and reasoning shown
- E.5 — Specific examples/practical application of mathematics presented
- E.6 — All calculations necessary to find the answer; each step results in further development towards the aim
- E.7 — Mathematics error-free and uses appropriate approximation at all times
